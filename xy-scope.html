X-Y Scope

Left channel moves the beam sideways, right channel moves it up and down. What you see here is exactly what plays out of the headphone jack.

Two-channel generator

Traced shape

The beam traces the whole outline once per cycle, so the trace rate is the note you hear. At 110 Hz the shape is drawn 110 times a second and plays an A2. Raise the pitch and the same picture sounds higher.

Draw your own shape

Draw on the screen with your finger or the mouse, in as many strokes as you like, or type something and tap Write to have the beam spell it out (long text wraps onto up to four lines). Writing replaces the drawing, and Undo brings it back. The beam traces every line once per cycle and darts between separate strokes, so the trace rate is still the note you hear, and you can draw while the sound plays. Simple drawings stay sharp; busy ones get grainier at high pitch. Your drawing is kept on this device and also appears under Shapes, where it can spin or sit on top of a song.

Driving a real scope

  1. Headphone or line out to a 3.5 mm to 2× RCA cable, then RCA-to-BNC adapters. Left (white) into CH1, right (red) into CH2.
  2. Set the scope to X-Y, both channels to DC coupling at about 0.2 V/div, and start with the volume here and on the computer low.
  3. Turn off sound "enhancements", virtual surround and bass management. Anything that alters one channel bends the picture.
  4. Keep CRT brightness low. Stopping the sound leaves a still, bright dot, which burns the phosphor, so turn intensity down first.
